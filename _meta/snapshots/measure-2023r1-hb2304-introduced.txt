82nd OREGON LEGISLATIVE ASSEMBLY--2023 Regular Session
House Bill 2304
Sponsored by Representative LEWIS; Representatives CATE, LEVY B, OWENS (Presession filed.)
SUMMARY
The following summary  is not prepared by the sponsors of the measure and is not a part of the body thereof subject
to consideration by  the Legislative Assembly. It is an editor’s brief statement of the essential features of the
measure as introduced.
Directs Department of Public Safety Standards  and Training to establish program to provide
grants to nonprofit organization to fund care of retired law enforcement dogs.
Appropriates moneys from General Fund to department for purposes of awarding grants.
Declares emergency, effective on passage.
A BILL FOR AN ACT
Relating to retired law enforcement dogs; and declaring an emergency.
Be It Enacted by the People of the State of Oregon:
SECTION 1.
(1) The Department of Public Safety Standards and Training shall  establish
a program to provide grants to a nonprofit organization to fund  the care of retired law
enforcement dogs.
(2) The department may award grants under this section to a nonprofit organization  that
agrees to consult on applications for funding with:
(a) A representative of the Oregon Police Canine Association;
(b) A representative of the veterinary industry; and
(c) A nonprofit organization that advocates for working dogs.
(3) A nonprofit organization awarded a grant under this section  may provide to a care-
taker of a retired law enforcement dog  that meets the requirements of subsection (4) of this
section:
(a) Up to $1,000 per year to reimburse the caretaker for the costs of  daily care and sup-
plies for the retired law enforcement dog; and
(b) Up to $5,000 to reimburse the caretaker for emergency veterinary  care for the retired
law enforcement dog.
(4) A caretaker of a retired law  enforcement dog is eligible to receive funding under the
program established in  this section if the law enforcement agency that used the law
enforcement dog certifies that:
(a) The retired law enforcement dog was  medically retired from a law enforcement
agency in this state due to an on-the-job injury or was used by a law  enforcement agency in
this state for at least 36 months;
(b) The retired law enforcement dog was certified in patrol,  drug detection or explosives
detection; and
(c) The law enforcement agency will notify the  nonprofit organization when the law
enforcement dog dies.
