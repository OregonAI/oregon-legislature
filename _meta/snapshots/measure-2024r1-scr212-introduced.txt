SUMMARY
The following summary  is not prepared by the sponsors of the measure and is not a part of the body thereof subject
to consideration by  the Legislative Assembly. It is an editor’s brief statement of the essential features of the
measure as introduced. The statement includes a measure digest written in compliance with applicable readability

Digest: Honors the life and memory of Clifford Trow. (Flesch Readability Score: 71.8).
In memoriam: Clifford Trow, 1929-2023.
CONCURRENT RESOLUTION
Whereas Clifford W. Trow was born in Topeka, Kansas, on July 27, 1929; and
Whereas in 1951, Clifford  Trow graduated with high honors from Kansas Wesleyan University
and promptly embarked upon a career as a high school social studies teacher; and
Whereas Clifford Trow resumed his schooling later in the decade, graduating  with a Master of
Arts from  the University of Colorado in 1958, and eight years later he completed his PhD, also from
the University of Colorado; and
Whereas following  the completion of his PhD, Clifford Trow moved to Oregon, where he assumed
a position on the history faculty at Oregon State University; and
Whereas as an academic, Clifford Trow’s research interests included the presidency  of Woodrow
Wilson as well as American foreign policy in Mexico and elsewhere; and
Whereas not long after his arrival in Corvallis, Oregon, in  1965, Clifford Trow met Jo Anne
Johnson, and in 1969 the couple married; and
Whereas in 1972, Clifford Trow received the Binkley-Stephenson Award  from the Organization
of American Historians for his paper “Woodrow Wilson and the Mexican  Interventionist Movement
of 1919,” published in the Journal of American History; and
Whereas Clifford Trow remained on staff at Oregon State University for more than thirty years,
retiring in 1996; and
Whereas Clifford Trow was active in Democratic Party politics from his days  in Colorado, and
he ran for the Oregon Senate in 1970, losing the District 18 race to incumbent Dick Hoyt; and
Whereas undeterred, Clifford Trow ran again four years later, this time winning the Senate  seat
and beginning a period of  service in the Oregon Senate that would last for nearly three decades
until his retirement in 2003; and
Whereas as a steadfast  supporter of education, Clifford Trow served on the Senate Education
Committee for eight sessions, including five as chair; and
Whereas in 1979, Clifford Trow was recognized as Education Senator of the Year by the  Oregon
Education Association, and  was similarly honored as Senator of the Year by the Oregon Library
Association in both 1991 and 1993; and
Whereas Clifford Trow was a member of numerous  additional legislative committees, chairing
Senate Ways and Means in 1993 and subcommittees of Ways and Means from 1985 to 1991; and

Whereas Clifford Trow was elected President Pro Tempore  of the Oregon Senate in 1981 and
served as Senate Democratic Leader from 1995 to 1998; and
Whereas in 2002, on the  eve of his retirement from elected office, Clifford Trow was honored
by the City of Corvallis, which declared December 16th to be “Cliff Trow Day”; and
Whereas Clifford Trow and his beloved wife, Jo Anne, remained active  in retirement, devoting
their energies to numerous civic  groups including Oregon State University’s Academy for Lifelong
Learning, which he cofounded, as well as the Benton  County Historical Society and the Old Mill
Center for Children and Families; and
Whereas Clifford Trow passed away on October 31, 2023; and
Whereas Clifford Trow is remembered as a man of  principle and integrity, as a devoted and
supportive husband, as a committed champion  for social justice, and as a model of a dedicated public
servant who left the world a better place; now, therefore,
Be It Resolved by the Legislative Assembly of the State of Oregon:
That we,  the members of the Eighty-second Legislative Assembly, recognize and honor Clifford
W. Trow for his lifetime of service and dedication to the people of  the State of Oregon; and be it
further
Resolved, That a copy of this resolution shall be presented to the family  of Clifford W. Trow as
an expression of our sympathy and condolences.
