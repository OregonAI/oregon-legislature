83rd OREGON LEGISLATIVE ASSEMBLY--2025 Regular Session
Senate Bill 1073
Sponsored by Senator NASH
SUMMARY
The following summary  is not prepared by the sponsors of the measure and is not a part of the body thereof subject
to consideration by  the Legislative Assembly. It is an editor’s brief statement of the essential features of the
measure as introduced. The statement includes a measure digest written in compliance with applicable readability
standards.
Digest: The Act  creates a program to train people to provide certain home health services to
their family members. (Flesch Readability Score: 66.3).
Establishes the Family Home Health Aide Program in the Oregon Health Authority  for the
purpose of certifying  family caregivers as family home health aides to provide qualified home health
services to eligible  relatives in the state medical assistance program. Requires the authority to re-
port on the outcomes of the program every two years.
Takes effect on the 91st day following adjournment sine die.
A BILL FOR AN ACT
Relating to family home health aides; and prescribing an effective date.
Whereas the home health workforce is facing a significant shortage of direct care  workers, in-
cluding home health aides, personal care aides and private duty nurses; and
Whereas in addition to a shortage  of direct care workers, the demand for private duty nursing
services remains high  due to an increasing desire for individuals to receive care in their community
rather than in an institutional setting; and
Whereas many families are turning to family  caregivers to fill the gap in care resulting from a
shortage of available direct care workers and long waiting lists for care; and
Whereas private duty nursing services require caregivers with proper  education, licensing and
credentials to allow for the highest standard of care in the home; now, therefore,
Be It Enacted by the People of the State of Oregon:
SECTION 1.
(1) As used in this section:
(a) “Coordinated care organization” has the meaning given that term in ORS 414.025.
(b) “Eligible relative” means an individual of any age who:
(A) Has a physical, mental  or cognitive impairment that prevents the individual from
safely living independently; and
(B) Is eligible to receive skilled nursing services under the  state medical assistance pro-
gram.
(c) “Family caregiver” means a parent, guardian  or family member related by
consanguinity to an eligible relative who:
(A) Is able, based  on physical proximity, to provide appropriate and consistent care to the
eligible relative; and
(B) Has the necessary physical and mental capacity to  assist the eligible relative with
qualified home health services.
(d) “Family home health aide” means a family caregiver who has been certified under this
section to provide qualified home health services to an eligible relative.
(e) “Home care provider” means:

(B) An entity that provides private duty nursing services.
(f) “Medical assistance” has the meaning given that term in ORS 414.025.
(g) “Qualified home health services” means the  home health or private duty nursing
services that a family home health aide  certified under this section is authorized to provide
to an eligible relative.
(2) The Family Home Health Aide Program is established in the Oregon Health Authority
for the  purpose of certifying a family caregiver as a family home health aide to provide
qualified home health services to an eligible relative.
(3)(a) The authority, in consultation with the Oregon State Board of Nursing  and relative
stakeholders, shall develop a training course, including hands-on experience, for a family
caregiver to become certified as a family home health aide. The authority  shall ensure that
a family caregiver that participates in the training course is trained in  skills that are rele-
vant to the eligible relative’s plan of care and clinical acuity.  The training course may in-
clude:
(A) Patient care transfers;
(B) Assistance with activities of daily living;
(C) Normal range of motion and position;
(D) Adequate nutrition and fluid intake;
(E) Basic infection prevention and control procedures;
(F) Observation and reporting of a patient’s vital signs, blood pressure  and general pa-
tient status;
(G) Urinary catheterization and ostomy care;
(H) Ambulation;
(I) Wound care;
(J) Enteral care;
(K) Medication administration; and
(L) Any additional tasks, as determined by the authority,  in consultation with the board.
(b) The  authority shall provide, or may designate and reimburse selected home care
providers to provide, the training course developed under this subsection.
(4) The authority, in consultation with the board, shall certify a family  caregiver as a
family home health aide if the family caregiver:
(a) Is at least 18 years of age;
(b) Has successfully completed the training developed under this section; and
(c) Has successfully fulfilled any additional requirements, including education or compe-
tency requirements, established by the authority.
(5) Notwithstanding any  other provision of law, a home care provider may employ a
family home health aide to provide qualified home health services to an eligible relative.  A
home care provider that employs a family home health aide shall:
(a) Ensure that the family home health aide receives, at least every  90 days, appropriate
clinical supervision based on the clinical acuity of the eligible relative, which may be deter-
mined using the acuity tool described in subsection (9) of this section.
(b) Recertify an eligible relative’s plan of care at least every 180 days.
(6) Subject to the limitations described  in subsection (7) of this section, a family home
health aide is authorized to provide the following qualified home health services:

(7) A family home health aide that provides  qualified home health services to an eligible
relative:
(a) Must be employed by a home care provider;
(b) Must provide qualified home health services in accordance with the eligible  relative’s
plan of care; and
(c) May  not provide tracheostomy care or ventilation therapy or care, regardless of the
clinical acuity of the eligible relative, unless the family home health aide is a  nurse licensed
under ORS  chapter 678 or is otherwise licensed, certified or authorized by the laws of this
state to provide such therapy or care.
(8) To ensure continuity of care, the authority shall ensure that:
(a) An eligible relative who  receives qualified home health services from a family home
health aide remains enrolled in any other  home health or private duty nursing services for
which the eligible relative is eligible;
(b) An eligible relative with a higher clinical acuity, including an eligible relative  who
requires tracheostomy care or ventilation therapy or care,  has timely access to any neces-
sary home health or private duty nursing services; and
(c) A home care provider  that employs a family home health aide to provide qualified
home health services to an eligible relative is given preference to  provide any other neces-
sary home health or private duty nursing  services to the eligible relative, to the extent
practicable.
(9)(a) The authority may, in collaboration with home care providers that provide  services
to medical assistance recipients, medical assistance recipients, patient advocacy groups  and
other relevant stakeholders, develop a  clinical acuity tool to assist in determining the care

quality, clinically appropriate home health services, private  duty nursing services and quali-
fied home health services.
(b) If the authority develops a clinical acuity tool under this subsection, the authority
may use the clinical  acuity tool to conduct an independent assessment of whether an eligible

(10) The authority shall establish a program to provide respite care to family home health
aides, private duty nurses and home health aides. To  ensure continuity of care, the authority
shall ensure that any respite care is provided to a family home health aide,  private duty
nurse or  home health aide by the home care provider that employes the family home health
aide, private duty nurse or home health aide.
(11)(a) The authority and a coordinated care organization  shall provide reimbursement
in the state medical assistance program for up to 40 hours per week of  qualified home health
services provided by a family home health aide. The  authority shall establish fee-for-service
reimbursement rates  for qualified home health services that take into account any clinical
supervision that a home care provider must provide to a family home health aide.
(b) A coordinated care organization shall reimburse a home care provider  for qualified
home health services provided by a  family home health aide at the rates established in par-
agraph (a) of this subsection.
(c) The authority shall require a family home health aide  to submit claims for re-
imbursement in accordance  with the electronic visit verification requirements for providers
of services.
(12) At least once every two  years, the authority shall submit a report, in the manner
provided in ORS 192.245, to the Legislative Assembly and make the report available  on the
authority’s website. The report shall include:
(a) The number of hospitalizations and emergency department visits of eligible relatives
receiving qualified home health services through the program established under this section;
(b) For each eligible relative receiving qualified home health services under this section:
(A) The number of hours of private duty nursing services that the eligible  relative has
received and analysis of whether that number has increased  or decreased since the eligible
relative began receiving qualified home health services under this section; and
(B) The number of hours of qualified home health services that the eligible relative  has
received;
(c) The number of overpayments made  to home care providers that employ family home
health aides and any other evidence of fraud,  waste or abuse in the program established
under this section; and
(d) The impact of the program established under this section  on access to home health
and private duty nursing services for medical assistance recipients.
(13) The authority, in consultation with the  board, may adopt rules necessary to imple-
ment this section.
SECTION 2.
(1) Section 1 of this 2025 Act becomes operative on January 1, 2026.
(2) The Oregon Health Authority may take any  action before the operative date specified
in subsection (1) of this section that is necessary  to enable the authority to exercise, on and
after the operative date specified in  subsection (1) of this section, all of the duties, functions
and powers conferred on the authority by section 1 of this 2025 Act.
SECTION 3. This 2025 Act takes effect on the 91st day after the date on  which the 2025
regular session of the Eighty-third Legislative Assembly adjourns sine die.
