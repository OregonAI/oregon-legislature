## Full text

83rd OREGON LEGISLATIVE ASSEMBLY--2025 Regular Session
Senate Bill 933
Sponsored by Senator TAYLOR (at the request of Margaret DeLacy, Oregon Association for Talented and Gifted)
SUMMARY
The following summary  is not prepared by the sponsors of the measure and is not a part of the body thereof subject
to consideration by  the Legislative Assembly. It is an editor’s brief statement of the essential features of the
measure as introduced. The statement includes a measure digest written in compliance with applicable readability
standards.
Digest: Requires ODE to prepare state report cards  about school districts’ TAG students.
(Flesch Readability Score: 67.7).
Requires school districts to submit specified information to the Department  of Education related
to talented  and gifted children. Directs the department to issue annual district and state report
cards based on the submitted information.
A BILL FOR AN ACT
Relating to reports on talented and gifted children; creating new provisions; and  amending ORS
329.105 and 329.115.
Be It Enacted by the People of the State of Oregon:
SECTION 1.
Section 2 of this  2025 Act is added to and made a part of ORS 343.407 to
343.413.
SECTION 2. (1) At the time  and in the manner prescribed by the Department of Educa-
tion, each school district shall submit to the department information to enable  the depart-
ment to issue the annual report cards described in subsections (2) and (3) of this section.
(2) For each school district, the department shall  issue an annual report card that de-
scribes for the school district:
(a) The total number of  talented and gifted children and the percentage of talented and
gifted children, as compared to the total population.
(b) The total number of talented and gifted children and the  percentage of talented and
gifted children, as compared to the total population and  to other talented and gifted children,
in each of the following areas:
(A) General intellectual ability;
(B) Unusual academic ability in mathematics or language arts;
(C) Creative ability in using  original or nontraditional methods in thinking and producing;
(D) Leadership ability  in motivating the performance of others either in educational or
noneducational settings; and
(E) Ability in visual or performing arts.
(c) The total number of talented and gifted children and  the percentage of talented and
gifted children, as compared to the total population and  to other talented and gifted children,
in each of the following student groups:
(A) Ethnicity;
(B) Eligibility for English language learner programs;
(C) Whether the student has an individualized education  program or a plan developed in
accordance with section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act of 1973, 29 U.S.C. 794; and
NOTE: Matter in  boldfaced type in an amended section is new;  matter [ italic and bracketed] is existing law to be omitted.
New sections are in  boldfaced type.
LC 4177
(D) Whether the student is economically disadvantaged.
(d) The total number of talented and gifted children and the  percentage of talented and
gifted children, as compared to the total population and  to other talented and gifted children,
in each of the following grade groupings:
(A) Kindergarten through grade 2;
(B) Grades 3 through 5;
(C) Grades 6 through 8; and
(D) Grades 9 through 12.
(e) The total number of talented  and gifted children and the percentage of talented and
gifted children, as compared to the total population and  to other talented and gifted children,
who have regular attendance rates, as defined in  ORS 327.190, in each of the following stu-
dent groups:
(A) Ethnicity;
(B) Eligibility for English language learner programs;
(C) Whether the student has an individualized education  program or a plan developed in
accordance with section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act of 1973, 29 U.S.C. 794; and
(D) Whether the student is economically disadvantaged.
(f) The total number of talented and gifted children and the  percentage of talented and
gifted children, as compared to the total population and  to other talented and gifted children,
in kindergarten through grade eight who:
(A) Are enrolled in advanced classes; or
(B) Participate in programs during the school day that provide accelerated curriculum
and instruction.
(g) The total number of  talented and gifted children and the percentage of talented and
gifted children, as compared to the total population and  to other talented and gifted children,
in grades 9 through 12 who are enrolled in:
(A) Career and technical education courses;
(B) Accelerated college credit programs, as defined in ORS 340.300; and
(C) Alternative education programs, as defined in ORS 336.615.
(h) The number and difficulty  level of high school mathematics and science courses taken
by talented and gifted children, reported  based on identification as talented and gifted chil-
dren in each of the following areas:
(A) General intellectual ability;
(B) Unusual academic ability in mathematics; and
(C) Unusual academic ability in language arts.
(i) The number  and percentage of full-time equivalent positions that are allocated to the
instruction of talented and gifted children.
(j) The training and credentials of staff who have responsibilities related to:
(A) The administration of the instruction of talented and gifted education;
(B) The identification of talented and gifted children; and
(C) The instruction of talented and gifted children.
(k) The amount and percentage of total moneys  received under Title I of the federal El-
ementary and Secondary  Education Act of 1965 that are used for services for talented and
gifted children.
(L) The amount  and percentage of total moneys expended on instruction for talented and
gifted children.
(m) The average amount of moneys expended for each talented and gifted child.
(n) The results of  a parent survey on the adequacy of instruction provided to talented
and gifted children.
(o) The annual achievement test score gains in mathematics and in language arts for  all
enrolled students who took the statewide summative assessments in both the previous school
year and the current school year, disaggregated in the following order:
(A) By subject matter and grade;
(B) By proficiency level; and
(C) By demographic information identified by the State Board of  Education, which must
include, at a minimum, gender identity, racial or ethnic identity, whether the student is
economically disadvantaged and whether the  student is eligible for special education and re-
lated services.
(3) For the state, the department shall issue a report card that describes for the state:
(a) Each of the components described in subsection (2) of this section; and
(b) The percentage of students identified as talented and  gifted children for school dis-
tricts, based on whether the  school district is small, medium or large, as defined by the State
Board of Education by rule.
(4) The State  Board of Education may adopt any rules necessary for the administration
of this section, including limitations on the disclosure of information  to ensure that per-
sonally identifiable information about an individual student is not disclosed.

(b) Information about how to access  the website that is maintained by the State Department of
