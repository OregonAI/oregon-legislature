## Full text

83rd OREGON LEGISLATIVE ASSEMBLY--2025 Regular Session
Senate Bill 1159
Sponsored by Senators GELSER BLOUIN, MANNING JR; Senators BROADMAN, WEBER
SUMMARY
The following summary  is not prepared by the sponsors of the measure and is not a part of the body thereof subject
to consideration by  the Legislative Assembly. It is an editor’s brief statement of the essential features of the
measure as introduced. The statement includes a measure digest written in compliance with applicable readability
standards.
Digest: The  Act moves the office of Children’s Advocate from DHS to the LTCO, changes how
the CA is appointed and moves oversight of the CIRTs from DHS to  the CA. (Flesch Readability
Score: 66.4).
Transfers the office of Children’s Advocate  from the Department of Human Services to the office
of the Long Term Care Ombudsman. Establishes the Children’s Advocacy Advisory Board  to nomi-
nate candidates for the  Governor to select from when appointing the Children’s Advocate. Expands
the duties  and authority of the Children’s Advocate. Transfers oversight of the Critical Incident
Review Teams from the Director of Human Services to the Children’s Advocate.
Takes effect on the 91st day following adjournment sine die.
A BILL FOR AN ACT
Relating to the Children’s Advocate;  creating new provisions; amending ORS 21.007, 409.185, 417.805,
417.810, 417.815, 417.825, 418.201, 418.811, 418.813, 418.816, 419B.005 and 419B.035; and prescrib-
ing an effective date.
Be It Enacted by the People of the State of Oregon:
CHILDREN’S ADVOCATE
SECTION 1.
ORS 417.805, 417.810, 417.815 and 417.825  are added to and made a part of
sections 3 to 10 of this 2025 Act.
SECTION 2. Definitions. As used in sections 3 to 10 of this 2025 Act:
(1) “Child” means an individual under 21  years of age who is receiving child welfare ser-
vices or juvenile justice services.
(2) “Child-caring agency” has the meaning given that term in ORS 418.205.
SECTION 3.
Children’s Advocate Account.  The Children’s Advocate Account  is estab-
lished in the State Treasury, separate and distinct from the General  Fund. The Children’s
Advocate Account consists of moneys deposited in  the account under ORS 417.825 and may
include miscellaneous receipts, gifts and federal  and other grants received by the Children’s
Advocate and deposited to the account. The moneys in the account are continuously appro-
priated to the office of Children’s Advocate for carrying out the responsibilities of the  office
of Children’s Advocate.
SECTION 4. Investigations. (1) The Children’s Advocate may, in response to a complaint
or on the Children’s Advocate’s own initiative, investigate an administrative act affecting
children that the Children’s Advocate reasonably believes may be:
(a) Contrary to law;
(b) Based on mistaken facts or irrelevant considerations;
(c) Unsupported by an adequate statement of reasons;

New sections are in  boldfaced type.

(d) Performed in an inefficient manner;
(e) Unreasonable, unfair or otherwise objectionable, even  though in accordance with law;
(f) A risk to the health, safety, welfare or rights of a child; or
(g) Otherwise erroneous.
(2) The Children’s Advocate may decline to investigate a complaint if:
(a) The Children’s Advocate reasonably believes the  complaint can be resolved through
a different remedy or process;
(b) The Children’s Advocate has reasonable cause to believe the complaint is trivial,
frivolous, vexatious or not made in good faith;
(c) The complaint was delayed too  long to justify examination or the information received
by the Children’s Advocate is insufficient to initiate an investigation; or
(d) The Children’s Advocate lacks sufficient resources to conduct a full investigation.
(3) When  the Children’s Advocate declines to investigate a complaint, the Children’s Ad-
vocate is not precluded from proceeding, on the Children’s  Advocate’s own initiative, to in-
vestigate an administrative act or circumstance regardless  of whether it is included in a
complaint.
(4) When the Children’s advocate reasonably believes there is  an imminent risk of harm
to a child, the Children’s Advocate shall immediately  report the circumstances to the ap-
propriate authority that can provide for the child’s safety, regardless of whether the
Children’s Advocate chooses to investigate the  circumstances surrounding the imminent risk
of harm to the child.
(5)(a) The  Department of Human Services, Oregon Youth Authority, county juvenile de-
partment or other entity that is the subject of a  complaint, or a person acting in the inter-
ests of the department, authority, county juvenile department  or other entity, may not
retaliate against a person who in good faith files a complaint or participates in  an investi-
gation under this section, including retaliation by:
(A) The imposition of penalties, sanctions or restrictions against the person;
(B) Taking adverse actions connected with the person’s employment or placement; or
(C) The denial of a right, privilege or benefit otherwise available to the person.
(b) A person who alleges retaliation as described in  this subsection may commence a civil
action for appropriate  injunctive relief, damages or other appropriate relief. Upon prevailing
in an action brought under this paragraph, the plaintiff may recover special, general  and
punitive damages.  In addition, the court shall award reasonable attorney fees to the pre-
vailing plaintiff.
(c) The remedies provided by this subsection to a person who is an  employee are in ad-
dition to any remedy provided to an employee under ORS 659A.199.
SECTION 5.
Obstructing a Children’s Advocate investigation.  (1) A person  commits the
crime of obstructing a  Children’s Advocate investigation if the person knowingly and
willfully:
(a) Misleads or attempts to mislead the Children’s Advocate or a  designee of the
Children’s Advocate in the course of an investigation under section 4 of this 2025 Act; or
(b) Obstructs  or hinders the proper and lawful exercise of the Children’s Advocate’s
powers in the course of an investigation under section 4 of this 2025 Act.
(2) Obstructing a Children’s Advocate investigation is a Class A misdemeanor.
SECTION 6.
Procedures to maintain confidentiality. The Children’s Advocate shall es-
tablish procedures to maintain the confidentiality  of children’s records and files. These pro-
cedures must meet the following requirements:
(1) The Children’s Advocate or a designee of the Children’s Advocate may not  disclose
the identity of any child unless the child or the child’s legal representative consents  in
writing to the disclosure and specifies to whom the disclosure may be made.
(2) The identity of any complainant or child  on whose behalf a complaint is made, or in-
dividual providing information on behalf of the complainant or the child, shall be confidential
and is not subject to disclosure under ORS 192.311 to 192.478. If  the complaint becomes the
subject of judicial proceedings, at the request of the court, the Children’s  Advocate may
disclose the investigative information held by the Children’s Advocate or the designee.
SECTION 7.
Right of entry into  facilities and access to records.  (1) The Children’s Ad-
vocate and each designee of the Children’s Advocate has the right of  entry into any premises
or facility at which a child is being served or housed under the authorization, request  or di-
rection of  the Department of Human Services, the Oregon Youth Authority or a county ju-
venile department. The Children’s Advocate or designee may enter the premises  or facility
at any time considered necessary and reasonable by the Children’s Advocate  or the designee
if the entry is for the purpose of:
(a) Investigating and resolving complaints  made by children or on behalf of children in
the care or custody of the Department of Human  Services, the Oregon Youth Authority or
a county juvenile department or in the care or custody of a person, organization, program
or facility licensed, certified or otherwise authorized or contracted with to provide care  or
services to  children by the Department of Human Services, the Oregon Youth Authority or
a county juvenile department;
(b) Interviewing children, with their consent, in private;
(c) Offering the services of the Children’s Advocate to any eligible child, in private;
(d) Interviewing employees or agents of the facility, agency, program, person or organ-
ization;
(e) Consulting regularly  with the administrators of the facility, agency, program or or-
ganization; and
(f) Providing Children’s Advocate services authorized by law or by rule.
(2) The Children’s Advocate shall have access to a child’s  records and to records of a
public agency as necessary to carry out the duties of the  Children’s Advocate, including re-
cords on  reports of abuse, use of restraint or seclusion or licensing or contract violations.
The provisions of ORS 192.553 to 192.581 are  not intended to limit the access of the Children’s
Advocate to a child’s medical records. If necessary  to investigate a complaint, designees of
the Children’s Advocate shall have access to a child’s records, including medical records  as
authorized by the child or the child’s legal representative.
(3) The Children’s Advocate shall enter  into confidentiality agreements with the Depart-
ment of Human  Services, Oregon Youth Authority, Department of Education, Oregon Health
Authority, county juvenile  departments and public school districts, permitting the Children’s
Advocate to have  access to electronic records as necessary to carry out the duties of the
Children’s Advocate. The agreement must  ensure that records obtained by the Children’s
Advocate that are confidential, privileged or otherwise protected from disclosure are  not
further disclosed, except as permitted by state or federal law.
(4) The Children’s Advocate or designee shall ensure that entry into a facility or premises
as authorized by this section be done in a manner that does  not significantly disrupt the
providing of services to children.
(5) When seeking entry to a facility or premises under  this section, the Children’s Ad-
vocate or designee shall show identification to the person in charge.
(6) A child may refuse to communicate with the Children’s Advocate or designee, pro-
vided the refusal be made directly to the Children’s Advocate or designee  and not through
an intermediary.
(7) Unless declined by the child, the Children’s Advocate or designee  shall include the
child in planning any course of action to be taken on the child’s behalf.
SECTION 8.
Report after investigation; referral to other agencies. (1) Following an in-
vestigation, the Children’s Advocate or a  designee of the Children’s Advocate shall report
opinions or recommendations to the party or parties affected and, if  appropriate, shall at-
tempt to resolve the  complaint using informal techniques of mediation, conciliation and
persuasion. The  Children’s Advocate shall refer to an appropriate state agency complaints
of conditions adversely affecting children or  complaints of conditions threatening the safety
or well-being of children that cannot be resolved in the manner described in this section.
(2) The Children’s Advocate or designee  shall promote and publicize programs that pro-
mote the safety or emotional or physical well-being and full expression of children’s rights.
SECTION 9. Notice of complaint  procedures; posting. (1) The Children’s Advocate shall
prepare and distribute to the Department of Human Services, Oregon Youth Authority,
county juvenile departments and any other program deemed appropriate by  the Children’s
Advocate a written notice describing  the procedures to follow in making a complaint, in-
cluding the address and telephone number  of the Children’s Advocate and local designee, if
any, and a poster describing the Children’s Advocate and providing contact information.
(2) The Department of Human Services, Oregon Youth Authority, county juvenile  de-
partments and any other program shall  ensure that each facility, program or premises pro-
viding services to children posts the written notice and poster as required by this section in
conspicuous places in the facility, program or  premises in accordance with procedures pro-
vided by the Children’s Advocate.
(3) The  Department of Human Services, Oregon Youth Authority, county juvenile de-
partments and any other program  shall ensure that the written notice is provided to each
child receiving services and to each child’s legal representative upon entry to service, at each
placement change and at  other appropriate times and places such that the notice is provided
no less often than once each quarter.
SECTION 10.
Rules. In accordance with applicable provisions of  ORS chapter 183, the
office of the Children’s Advocate may adopt rules necessary for the administration of
sections 3 to 10 of this 2025 Act.
