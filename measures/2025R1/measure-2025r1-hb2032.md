## Full text

83rd OREGON LEGISLATIVE ASSEMBLY--2025 Regular Session
House Bill 2032
Sponsored by Representatives WRIGHT, BOICE, MCINTIRE, DIEHL; Representatives MANNIX,  OSBORNE,
RESCHKE, Senator WEBER (Presession filed.)
SUMMARY
The following summary  is not prepared by the sponsors of the measure and is not a part of the body thereof subject
to consideration by the Legislative Assembly. It is an editor’s brief statement of the essential features  of the
measure as introduced. The statement includes a measure digest written in compliance with applicable readability
standards.
Digest: Tells a business that hosts online matter than  can harm a child to check the ages of
those who  can get access to the matter and not give access to people who are under 18 years of
age. Lets people sue the business for not checking ages and giving  people under 18 years of age
access to matter than can harm a child. (Flesch Readability Score: 60.7).
Requires a commercial entity that engages in the  business of publishing or distributing sexual
material harmful to minors to verify  the ages of individuals to whom the commercial entity distrib-
utes or  makes available the sexual material harmful to minors. Specifies reasonable methods for age
verification and requires a person that verifies ages to destroy or delete information  that could
identify an individual or the individual’s address or  that could disclose other personal information
about the individual.
Imposes liability in an amount not to exceed $5,000 for  failure to comply with the age verifica-
tion requirement if the failure results in exposing an individual under the age of 18  years to sexual
material harmful to minors. Imposes liability in an amount not to exceed the greater of a plaintiff’s
actual damages or $5,000 for failure to destroy or delete information used in age verification.
A BILL FOR AN ACT
Relating to age verification requirements for access to sexual material harmful to minors.
Be It Enacted by the People of the State of Oregon:
SECTION 1.
(1) As used in this section:
(a) “Commercial entity” means a corporation, limited liability company, nonprofit corpo-
ration, partnership, limited liability partnership, limited partnership, business  trust or other
business entity engaged in commerce with individual residents of this state.
(b) “Distributing” means  issuing, selling, giving, providing, delivering, transferring,
transmuting, circulating or disseminating by any means in exchange for compensation.
(c) “Publishing” means communicating or making  available to another person informa-
tion by means of a publicly available Internet website.
(d) “Sexual material  harmful to minors” means material that depicts sexually explicit
conduct, as defined in ORS 163.665, or that:
(A) The average person applying contemporary community standards  would find, taking
the material as a whole and with respect to persons under 18 years of  age, is designed to
appeal or pander to the prurient interest;
(B) Depicts sexual images or conduct that, taken  as a whole, lacks serious literary, ar-
tistic, political or scientific value for persons under 18 years of age; or
(C) In a manner patently offensive with respect to  persons under 18 years of age exploits,
is devoted to or principally consists of depictions or descriptions,  whether actual, simulated
or animated, of:
(i) A person’s pubic hair, anus or genitals or the nipple of the female breast; or
(ii) Touching, caressing or fondling of nipples, breasts, buttocks, anuses or genitals.
NOTE: Matter in  boldfaced type in an amended section is new;  matter [ italic and bracketed] is existing law to be omitted.
New sections are in  boldfaced type.
LC 145
(e) “Transactional data” means a sequence of information that documents an exchange,
agreement or transfer between an individual, commercial entity or third party used for the
purpose of satisfying a request or event, including records of a mortgage, education or em-
ployment.
(2)(a) A commercial entity that knowingly and intentionally engages in  the business of
publishing or  distributing material on an Internet website, including a social media platform,
more than one-third of which is sexual material harmful to minors, shall use a  reasonable
method to verify the  ages of persons to whom the commercial entity distributes or makes
available the material on the website and may  not distribute or make available to persons
under the age of 18 years any sexual material harmful to minors.
(b) A commercial entity that performs the verification described in paragraph (a) of  this
subsection may not store or retain, and shall delete or  destroy, any information that the
commercial entity used in performing the verification that could identify an individual  or the
individual’s address or contact  information or that could reveal other personal information
about the individual.
(c) A commercial  entity may agree with another person to have the other person perform
the verification described in paragraph (a) of this subsection on  behalf of the commercial
entity. The commercial entity in any agreement with the other person shall require the other
person to comply with the same requirements that apply to the commercial entity  under
paragraphs (a) and (b) of this subsection.
(3) A reasonable method to verify age under subsection (2)(a) of this section is:
(a) Using information stored on a  digital network that positively identifies individuals and
the birth dates of the individuals in a manner  that is minimally sufficient for the purposes
of authorizing payment in a commercial transaction or for the purposes of receiving benefits
from a governmental agency; or
(b) Complying with the requirements of a commercial system that verifies age using:
(A) Government-issued identification; or
(B) Public or private transactional data.
(4)(a) A commercial entity  that fails to verify the age of an individual to whom the
commercial entity distributes or makes available sexual material harmful to minors  is liable
for damages in  an amount not to exceed $5,000 for each instance in which the commercial
entity distributes or makes available sexual material harmful to minors to  an individual un-
der 18 years of age.
(b) A commercial entity or person that  fails to destroy or delete information the com-
mercial entity or person uses to perform  an age verification in accordance with the re-
quirements of subsection (2)(b) of this  section is liable for damages in an amount not to
exceed the greater of a plaintiff’s actual damages or $5,000.
(c) A plaintiff may bring an action under this subsection in a circuit court of this state
and may obtain a judgment for costs and attorney fees if the plaintiff prevails in the  action.
