83rd OREGON LEGISLATIVE ASSEMBLY--2025 Regular Session
House Bill 3255
Sponsored by Representative EVANS
SUMMARY
The following summary  is not prepared by the sponsors of the measure and is not a part of the body thereof subject
to consideration by  the Legislative Assembly. It is an editor’s brief statement of the essential features of the
measure as introduced. The statement includes a measure digest written in compliance with applicable readability
standards.
Digest: Says that an online business must have a telephone number and  electronic mail address
to hear and respond to customer concerns. Lets the Secretary of State check  to see if the business
obeys the requirement and to fine a business that does not obey. Says that the business can be
barred from this state if it fails to comply again and again. (Flesch Readability Score: 61.3).
Prohibits an online business from operating in this state unless the online business maintains a
permanent telephone number and electronic mail address that  allows customers to contact the on-
line business with concerns and receive  a timely and substantive response. Permits the Secretary
of State to test compliance with the Act and to impose civil penalties  for a failure to comply or to
administratively  dissolve  an online business or revoke the online business’s authority to transact
business in this state if the  Secretary of State determines that the failure to comply is intentional,
willful and repeated.
A BILL FOR AN ACT
Relating to requirements for online businesses to provide contact methods  to respond to customer
concerns.
Be It Enacted by the People of the State of Oregon:
SECTION 1.
Section 2 of this 2025 Act is added to and made a part of ORS chapter 56.
SECTION 2. (1) As used in this section:
(a) “Customer” means  a natural person who resides in this state and purchases a product
or service from an online business.
(b) “Online business” means a person that engages in the business  of selling goods or
services to customers and 50 percent or more of the  sales occur entirely by electronic means
or by means of an Internet platform, website or other online presence.
(2)(a) An online business  may not sell goods or services to a customer of this state unless
the online business maintains a permanent telephone  number and electronic mail address by
which the customer may contact  and receive a timely response from the online business with
respect to the customer’s  concerns, of whatever nature, about the product or service the
online business sold to the customer or about the online business’s operations or  conduct
with respect to the customer.
(b) For the purposes of paragraph  (a) of this subsection, a timely response means a re-
sponse that directly addresses a customer’s expressed concern in  a commercially reasonable
amount of time, but not later than 24 hours  after a customer initiates contact with the on-
line business, except that an online business may respond substantively  to a customer’s
concern later than 24 hours after the initial  contact if responding to the concern requires
an unusual or extraordinary effort, research or  investigation or if circumstances beyond the
control of the online business prevent the timely response.
(c) If an exception described in paragraph (b) of this  subsection occurs or is likely, an

online business shall respond to a customer during  the initial contact by stating that the
online business cannot respond immediately and giving the customer an estimate of when the
online business will respond substantively to the customer’s concern.
(3) The Secretary of State may test an  online business’s compliance with the require-
ments of subsection (2) of this section randomly and without prior notice and may  impose a
civil penalty in an amount the Secretary  of State determines by rule if the Secretary of State
determines that the online business has not complied. The rules  must require an escalating

(4) If the Secretary of State determines that an  online business intentionally, willfully
