## Summary

**Catchline:** Requires each electric company to develop a distributed power plant program for the procurement of grid services to be provided by distributed energy resources.

**Relating to:** Relating to energy; prescribing an effective date.

Digest: Makes each power company create a program for buying grid services. (Flesch Readability Score: 64.9). 
 Requires each electric company to develop a distributed power plant program for the procurement of grid services to be provided by distributed energy resources. Specifies the grid services that may be eligible for compensation and the classes of distributed energy resources technologies that must be eligible to provide the grid services. Allows a customer to enroll directly or through a third party in an electric company's distributed power plant program. 
 Requires an electric company to use a standard offer, open access tariff. Allows an electric company to recover in rates prudently incurred costs associated with the program. 
 Directs the Public Utility Commission to develop and adopt annual procurement targets and performance incentives. Allows an electric company to earn performance incentives for meeting program targets. 
 Requires each electric company to file an annual report with the commission on the status of the electric company's distributed power plant program, including the capacity enrolled in the program. 
 Provides for initial requirements and an implementing timeline. Requires an initial distributed power plant program to provide compensation for system-wide peak load reduction that is provided by an energy storage device that is a battery. 
 Takes effect on the 91st day following adjournment sine die.

83rd OREGON LEGISLATIVE ASSEMBLY--2026 Regular Session
Senate Bill 1582
Sponsored by Senator NERON MISSLIN,  Representatives GAMBA, ANDERSEN, MCDONALD, RIEKE SMITH;
Senators FREDERICK, GOLDEN, PATTERSON, PHAM K, REYNOLDS, SOLLMAN, Representatives
CHAICHI, HELM, SOSA, WALTERS (Presession filed.)
SUMMARY
The following summary  is not prepared by the sponsors of the measure and is not a part of the body thereof subject
to consideration by  the Legislative Assembly. It is an editor’s brief statement of the essential features of the
measure as introduced. The statement includes a measure digest written in compliance with applicable readability
standards.
Digest: Makes each power company create a program for buying grid services. (Flesch Read-
ability Score: 64.9).
Requires each electric company to  develop a distributed power plant program for the procure-
ment of grid services to be provided by distributed energy resources. Specifies the  grid services that
may be eligible for compensation and the classes of distributed energy resources  technologies that
must be eligible to provide the grid services. Allows a customer to enroll  directly or through a third
party in an electric company’s distributed power plant program.
Requires an electric  company to use a standard offer, open access tariff. Allows an electric
company to recover in rates prudently incurred costs associated with the program.
Directs  the  Public Utility Commission to develop and adopt annual procurement targets and
performance incentives. Allows an electric company to earn performance incentives for meeting
program targets.
Requires each electric company to file  an annual report with the commission on the status of
the electric company’s distributed power plant program, including the  capacity enrolled in the pro-
gram.
Provides for initial requirements and an implementing timeline. Requires an initial distributed
power plant program to provide compensation  for system-wide peak load reduction that is provided
by an energy storage device that is a battery.
Takes effect on the 91st day following adjournment sine die.
A BILL FOR AN ACT
Relating to energy; and prescribing an effective date.
Whereas distributed power plants are dynamic  load management and energy supply resources
that support grid operations, reduce costs to ratepayers and achieve important  public policy goals;
and
Whereas distributed power plants can reduce  demand for electricity during peak periods, shift
electricity  consumption  out of peak periods, make renewable energy generated during off-peak peri-
ods available for use during on-peak periods, supply energy to the electric grid at  desired times,
provide frequency regulation, voltage  support and ancillary services, improve the resiliency and re-
liability of the electric system and provide other grid services; and
Whereas distributed  power plants can facilitate and optimize the utilization of electricity gen-
erated from wind and solar to help  utilities increase hosting capacity and integrate more renewable
energy resources; and
Whereas distributed power  plants can reduce costs to ratepayers by utilizing customer-sited re-
sources to provide grid services, avoiding or reducing reliance on  fossil fuel power plants to meet
peak demand, avoiding or deferring the need to  construct new and more costly grid-scale resources,
optimizing the use of existing assets and avoiding or deferring distribution and transmission  system
upgrades and other grid investments; and
Whereas distributed power plants can promote equity by  reducing costs for all ratepayers, ex-

Be It Enacted by the People of the State of Oregon:
SECTION 1.
Sections 2 to 5 of this 2026 Act are added to and  made a part of ORS chapter
757.
SECTION 2. Definitions. As used in this section and sections 3 and 4 of this 2026 Act:
(1) “Aggregator” means an entity that is identified as having responsibility, in the  pro-
vision of grid services, for coordinating the operations of  enrolled devices of customers who,
through the entity, enroll and participate  in an electric company’s distributed power plant
program.
(2) “Behind-the-meter” means the customer’s side of an electric meter.
(3) “Customer” means a person who has an active account for electricity services  with
an electric company.
(4) “Distributed energy resource” means an energy  system or device that is located
behind-the-meter and that generates or stores energy or provides energy management ca-
pabilities.
(5) “Distributed power plant” means an aggregation of distributed energy resources  that
operate in coordination to provide one or more grid services.
(6) “Electric company” has the meaning given that term in ORS 757.600.
(7) “Energy storage device” means a device that can store electrical energy at one point
or period of time for use at a later point or period of time.
(8) “Enrolled device” means  a distributed energy resource that is enrolled in a distributed
power plant program.
(9) “Grid service” means an energy, capacity or ancillary service that supports  electric
grid operations and is provided by one or more distributed energy resources.
(10) “Zero emission electricity” means electricity that is generated and may be stored  in
a manner that does not emit greenhouse gas into the atmosphere.
SECTION 3.
Distributed power plant program. (1)(a) Each electric company shall develop
and file with the Public Utility Commission  a distributed power plant program, consistent
with the provisions of this section and section 4 of this 2026 Act, for the procurement  of grid
services from customers of the electric company who enroll in the program.
(b) Within 120 days of an electric company filing a proposed distributed  power plant
program or an  amendment to a distributed power plant program, the commission shall deny,
approve or modify and approve the proposed program or amendment to the program.
(c) In its review of an electric company’s proposed distributed  power plant program or
amendment to a distributed power  plant program, the commission shall provide an opportu-
nity for, and take into consideration, input from the public and stakeholders.
(2) Under an electric company’s distributed power plant program, the electric  company
shall:
(a) Offer customers of the electric company a  standard offer, open access tariff for grid
services that are provided by distributed energy resources; and
(b) Develop and  use a schedule of compensation and terms that is based on the grid
service provided and the class of distributed  energy resource technology that is providing the
grid service.
(3) Grid services that are eligible for compensation  under a standard offer, open access
tariff that is offered under an electric company’s distributed power plant program  may in-
clude:
(a) System-wide peak load reduction;
(b) Local peak demand reduction;
(c) The provision of zero emission electricity to meet peak demand;
(d) Avoidance or deferral of  transmission or distribution upgrades or capacity expansion
and other location-specific grid services;
(e) Voltage support for maintaining stable voltage levels in the electric grid;
(f) Emergency services; and
(g) Any other grid  services determined by the commission to support efficient planning
or operation of the electric grid.
(4) The classes of distributed energy resources  technologies that may provide grid ser-
vices under an electric company’s distributed power plant program must include, but  are not
limited to, the following:
(a) Energy storage devices that are a battery;
(b) Load control devices, including but  not limited to, smart thermostats, water heaters
and other load control devices approved by the commission; and
(c) Electric vehicles.
(5) A distributed power plant program must  specify the operational parameters for each
grid service based on  the class of distributed energy resource technology that provides the
grid service. At a minimum, operational parameters must include:
(a) The minimum and maximum number of times an enrolled  device may be dispatched
in a 12-month period;
(b) The months of the year, days of the  week and times of the day that an enrolled device
may be dispatched;
(c) The maximum duration for which an enrolled device may be dispatched; and
(d) The notification requirements for when an enrolled device will  be dispatched, except
in response to an emergency event.
(6) A customer of an electric company may enroll:
(a) Directly or through an aggregator  in the electric company’s distributed power plant
program.
(b) For as many  grid services and as many distributed energy resource devices that are
provided for under an electric company’s  distributed power plant program. A customer may
only enroll a distributed energy resource device  that the customer uses for the customer’s
personal benefit,  but the device may be owned or maintained by the customer or a third
party.
(7)(a) If a customer enrolls directly  in an electric company’s distributed power plant
program, the electric  company shall provide the customer an upfront payment and per-
formance compensation for each  distributed energy resource device the customer enrolls in
the program.
(b) The electric company may not require, as a condition to participating  in the program,
a customer to install an additional meter or provide a security deposit or collateral.
(c) An electric company is allowed to communicate with, send a dispatch  signal to or
verify the performance of any  enrolled device consistent with the terms of the electric
company’s distributed power plant  program and any standards or requirements adopted by
the commission.
(d) An electric company may disenroll a customer or an enrolled device  after one year
of participation for  repeated nonperformance of the enrolled device. Prior to disenrolling a
customer or a device, during the course of the year, the electric  company shall provide suf-
ficient notice and opportunity to cure the nonperformance. An electric company may not
assess a penalty for the nonperformance of an enrolled device.
(e) An electric company may adopt reasonable requirements as approved  by the com-
mission for participating in the electric company’s distributed power plant program.
(8)(a) If a  customer enrolls through an aggregator in an electric company’s distributed
power plant program, the electric company shall follow the provisions of subsection  (7) of
this section except the  electric company shall work with, communicate through and provide
all payments and compensation to the aggregator.
(b) An aggregator shall be responsible for:
(A) Managing the participation of the customers whom the aggregator enrolls;
(B) Maintaining good communication with the electric company;
(C) Receiving dispatch signals from the electric company;
(D) Communicating directly with and managing the performance of  the devices enrolled
through the aggregator in the distributed power plant program;
(E) Providing the electric company with data needed to  verify the performance of the
devices enrolled through the aggregator in the distributed power plant program; and
(F) Receiving payments and compensation from the electric company.
(c) An aggregator shall be considered a participant of the program.
(d) An electric company or an affiliate  of an electric company may not be an aggregator.
(9) An electric company’s distributed power  plant program may provide for a higher up-
front payment for a class of customers that is based  on the differential energy burdens on
low- and moderate-income customers  and other economic, social equity or environmental
justice factors that affect affordability for certain classes of utility customers.
(10) Under an electric company’s distributed  power plant program, a customer or
aggregator shall be given the  option to enter into an agreement that sets the performance
compensation rate for a period of five years or longer. The  option must be exercised by the
customer or aggregator at the time the customer,  whether directly or through an
aggregator, enrolls or renews enrollment in the program.
(11) A customer may  participate in an electric company’s distributed power plant pro-
gram in addition to participating in any other program offered  by the electric company. Any
payment or compensation  that a customer receives under an electric company’s distributed
power plant program shall be in addition to any  incentive, payment or credit that a customer
is entitled to receive under any other program in which the customer is participating, in-
cluding any net metering program established pursuant to ORS 757.300.
(12) A distributed energy resource that is owned by an electric company or an  affiliate
of an electric company may not be enrolled in an electric company’s distributed  power plant
program.
(13) An electric company  may contract with a third party to provide a distributed energy
resource management system to assist the electric company in implementing the electric
company’s distributed power plant program.
(14) All prudently incurred costs  associated with an electric company’s distributed power
plant program, including upfront payments and  performance payments, are recoverable in
the rates of an electric company.  The commission may allow a reasonable rate of return on
the performance payments made by an electric company for grid services provided under  the
electric company’s distributed power plant program.
SECTION 4.
Procurement targets; performance incentives; annual report. (1)(a) The
Public Utility Commission shall develop and adopt:
(A) Annual  procurement targets, for a five-year period, for the procurement of grid ser-
vices under a distributed power plant program approved under section 3 of this 2026  Act; and
(B) Annual performance incentives for achieving those procurement targets.
(b) The procurement  targets must require a meaningful annual increase in the amount
of grid services that an electric company procures under the electric  company’s distributed
power plant program.
(c) The performance incentives:
(A) Must include financial incentives for achieving procurement targets; and
(B) May include financial penalties for failing to achieve procurement targets.
(2) Prior to the expiration of a five-year period, the commission shall develop and adopt
annual procurement targets and performance incentives for  the subsequent five-year period.
(3) If an electric company adds a grid service to the electric company’s distributed  power
plant program tariff, the commission shall develop and adopt, within 270 days of  the electric
company adding the grid service, annual procurement targets and performance incentives for
the grid service.
(4) Annual procurement targets  and performance incentives that are adopted by the
commission for the procurement  of a grid service must take effect no later than January 1
of the year following the year in which the commission approves  the procurement of the grid
service under a distributed power plant program.
(5)(a) No later than January 31 of each year, each electric company shall file  a report
with the commission on the status of  the electric company’s distributed power plant pro-
gram. The report must include a description of:
(A) The total capacity enrolled in  the electric company’s distributed power plant pro-
gram;
(B) The capacity enrolled based on:
(i) Each grid service offered;
(ii) Each class of distributed energy resource technology;
(iii) Each customer class; and
(iv) Customers enrolled directly and customers enrolled through an aggregator;
(C) The number of events called during the calendar year for each grid service;
(D) The aggregate performance of each class of distributed energy resource  technology
for each grid service that a class provided; and
(E) By separate accounting, the total amount  of upfront payments and performance
payments issued for each grid service and each class of  distributed energy resource tech-
nology providing the grid service.
(b) The report shall include  recommendations for increasing participation in the electric
company’s distributed power plant program.
(c) The commission may  require a report to include additional information as the com-
mission finds necessary for evaluating the status of an electric company’s distributed  power
plant program.
SECTION 5.
Initial requirements; timeline. (1)(a) An electric company shall first file  with
the Public Utility Commission the electric company’s proposed distributed  energy resources
program under section 3 of this 2026 Act within  120 days of the effective date of this 2026
Act.
(b) At a minimum, the electric company’s initial distributed  power plant program must
provide for compensation for system-wide peak load reduction that is provided  by an energy
storage device that is a battery. For system-wide peak load reduction that is provided by an
energy storage device that is a battery,  the performance compensation shall be based on the
average capacity discharged from the device over the course of each event  during the appli-
cable capability period, measured directly at the device.
(c) Notwithstanding section 3 (4) of  this 2026 Act and except as provided in paragraph (d)
of this subsection, an electric company’s initial distributed power plant  program does not
need to provide for the following classes of distributed energy resources technologies:
(A) Load control devices; or
(B) Electric vehicles.
(d) Where feasible, an electric company shall incorporate any existing  programs offered
by the  electric company into the electric company’s distributed energy resources program,
such as smart thermostat demand response and electric vehicle charging programs.
(2) Notwithstanding section 3 (1)(b) of this 2026 Act, within 120 days of an electric com-
pany first filing with  the commission a proposed distributed energy resources program, the
commission may not deny a proposed distributed energy resources program  and shall either
approve or modify and approve the electric company’s proposed distributed energy resources
program.
(3) Notwithstanding section 4 (3) of this 2026 Act,  within 270 days of the effective date
of this 2026 Act, the commission shall develop  and adopt annual procurement targets and
performance incentives for system-wide peak load reduction that is provided by an  energy
storage device that is a battery.
(4) Within 12 months from the date that the commission approves  an electric company’s
initial distributed power plant program under subsection (2) of this  section, the electric
company shall file an amendment under section  3 of this 2026 Act to the electric company’s
distributed power plant program. The amendment must incorporate and provide for the fol-
lowing classes of distributed energy resources  technologies, to the extent the classes were
not included in the electric company’s initial distributed power plant program:
(a) Load control devices; and
(b) Electric vehicles.
(5) The provisions of this section  apply to an electric company that exists on the effective
date of this 2026 Act.
SECTION 6.
Captions. The section captions used in  this 2026 Act are provided only for
the convenience of the reader and do not become part of the statutory  law of this state or
express any legislative intent in the enactment of this 2026 Act.
SECTION 7. Effective date. This 2026 Act takes effect on the 91st day after the date on
which the 2026 regular session of the Eighty-third Legislative Assembly adjourns sine die.
