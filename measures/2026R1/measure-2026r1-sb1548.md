83rd OREGON LEGISLATIVE ASSEMBLY--2026 Regular Session
Senate Bill 1548
Printed pursuant to  Senate Interim Rule 213.28 by order of the President of the Senate in conformance with pre-
session filing rules,  indicating neither advocacy nor opposition on the part of the President (at the request
of Senate Interim Committee on Early Childhood and Behavioral Health)
SUMMARY
The following summary  is not prepared by the sponsors of the measure and is not a part of the body thereof subject
to consideration by  the Legislative Assembly. It is an editor’s brief statement of the essential features of the
measure as introduced. The statement includes a measure digest written in compliance with applicable readability
standards.
Digest: The Act says that cannabis edibles have to be in single unit packages and  says how
strong one edible can be. The Act also tells OLCC to make sure that cannabis items  include some
health information for the buyer. (Flesch Readability Score: 61.2).
Requires cannabinoid edibles to  be individually packaged and allows up to 10 milligrams of adult
use cannabinoid per individual cannabinoid  edible. Requires cannabinoid edibles and other
cannabinoid products to be packaged in a manner that is tailored judiciously to the specific purpose
of protecting minors from the negative health effects  of unlawfully using cannabinoids edibles and
other cannabinoid products. Allows local governments to establish buffer zones of  more than 1,000
feet around adult use cannabis and medical  marijuana operations in the interest of public health and
safety. Becomes operative January 1, 2027.
Requires the Oregon Liquor and Cannabis Commission to adopt rules to require  that marijuana
items and inhalant delivery systems that contain industrial hemp-derived cannabinoids bear a label,
or include an insert, describing certain risks of using marijuana items and inhalant delivery  systems
that contain industrial hemp-derived cannabinoids. Becomes operative July 1, 2027.
Takes effect on the 91st day following adjournment sine die.
A BILL FOR AN ACT
Relating to marijuana; creating new provisions; amending ORS  475C.017, 475C.449, 475C.604,
475C.612, 475C.620 and 475C.897; and prescribing an effective date.
Be It Enacted by the People of the State of Oregon:
SECTION 1.
Sections 2 and 3 of  this 2026 Act are added to and made a part of ORS
475C.600 to 475C.648.
SECTION 2. (1) In addition to the rules adopted under ORS  475C.604, the Oregon Liquor
and Cannabis Commission shall adopt rules to require that  marijuana items and inhalant
delivery systems that contain industrial  hemp-derived vapor items bear a label with the in-
formation described in subsection (2) of this section.
(2) The label required under subsection  (1) of this section must include the following in-
formation printed in at least a 12-point font size:
(a) A recommendation that new consumers begin with lower doses of cannabinoids;
(b) That care should be taken for the delayed  onset of cannabinoid edibles, including a
warning that it can take up to four hours to  feel the full effects from consuming cannabinoid
edibles and that consuming more within the four hours may  result in more adverse effects
that may require medical attention;
(c) The dangers of purchasing illegal  marijuana items or inhalant delivery systems that
contain industrial hemp-derived vapor items, including the increased risk that such products
are untested  and may contain unsafe additives or harmful contaminants such as mold or
pesticides;
(d) Warnings against  consuming or inhaling cannabinoids while pregnant or breastfeeding

and that exposure to  cannabis may harm the health of the fetus, including by causing low
birth weight;
(e) The potential for cannabis use to contribute to mental  health problems, including
psychotic disorders such  as schizophrenia, and increased suicide attempts and thoughts of
suicide, and that these risks are greatest for frequent users  and when using marijuana items
or inhalant delivery  systems containing industrial hemp-derived vapor items with higher
levels of tetrahydrocannabinol;
(f) The link between higher tetrahydrocannabinol  content and the likelihood of experi-
encing adverse effects and  impairment, including severe anxiety and the disruption of mem-
ory and concentration;
(g) Cautions that driving while under the influence of cannabinoids is unlawful and that
the use of cannabinoids when driving increases the risk of motor vehicle crashes;
(h) Evidence that starting cannabis use at a young age, or using  cannabis frequently,
may lead to problem use and may harm the developing brain; and
(i) That inhaling cannabis may make breathing problems worse  and that prolonged use
of cannabis inhalants may cause recurrent and severe nausea and vomiting.
(3) If the package of a marijuana item or inhalant delivery  system that contains an in-
dustrial hemp-derived vapor item is too small to bear a label described in subsection (2) of
this section, the marijuana item or inhalant delivery system that contains an industrial
hemp-derived vapor item must include a pamphlet or insert that bears the information de-
scribed in subsection (2) of this section. A marijuana retailer  licensed under ORS 475C.097
shall ensure that each consumer who purchases a  marijuana item or inhalant delivery sys-
tem that contains industrial  hemp-derived cannabinoids described in this subsection receives
the pamphlet or insert described in this subsection.
(4) Once every five years, the commission shall, in consultation with the  Oregon Health
Authority, evaluate the  requirements adopted by the commission by rule under this section
and may as necessary revise the requirements to ensure the requirements reflect the  cur-
rent state of the evolving science on the health effects of cannabis and on effective commu-
nication of health warnings.
(5) The commission may adopt rules as necessary to carry out this section.

occurring in the usable marijuana.
