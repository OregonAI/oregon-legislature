AN ACT

SECTION 1.
Sections 2 to 5 of this  2026 Act are added to and made a part of ORS 339.040
to 339.125.
SECTION 2. As used in sections 2 to 5 of this 2026 Act:
(1) “Enrollment” means attending classes and participating in extracurricular activities.
(2) “Fixed, regular and adequate nighttime residence” does not include:
(a) Sharing the housing of other persons due  to loss of housing, economic hardship or a
similar reason;
(b) Living in motels, hotels, trailer parks or camping grounds due to  the lack of alter-
native adequate accommodations;
(c) Living in emergency or transitional shelters;
(d) Living in a public or private place not designated for or ordinarily used as a  regular
sleeping accommodation for human beings; or
(e) Living in cars, parks, public spaces, abandoned buildings, substandard housing,  bus
or train stations or similar settings.
(3) “Homeless student” means a student who:
(a) Lacks a fixed, regular and adequate nighttime residence;
(b) Has been abandoned in a hospital; or
(c) Is living in any other circumstance under which  the student qualifies for support
under the McKinney-Vento Homeless Assistance Act, 42 U.S.C. 11431 et seq.
(4) “Local liaison” means a person designated by  a school district for the purposes de-
scribed in section 3 (2) of this 2026 Act.
(5) “Preschool” means an early childhood program:
(a) That serves children from birth through five years of age;
(b) That receives public funding; and
(c) For which a school district is accountable for providing  early childhood education
services or for which a school district is a financial or administrative agent.
(6) “School district” includes an education service district.

(7) “School of origin” means the preschool or public school:
(a) That a student attended before the student became a homeless student,  including a
school attended as provided by ORS 339.133  (5) or a public school attended voluntarily and

(b) In which the homeless student was last enrolled,  including a school attended as pro-

(c) That, for a student who completes the final grade level served by a school  described

following the school described in paragraph (a) or (b) of this subsection.
(8) “School of residence” means any public  school for which students who live within the
attendance boundary where the homeless student lives are eligible to attend, including a
public school attended voluntarily and for which there is no attendance boundary.
(9) “State coordinator” means a person designated by the Department of  Education for
the purposes described in section 5 of this 2026 Act.
(10) “Student” means:
(a) Any child who is eligible for preschool; or
(b) Any person that a district school board must admit as provided by ORS 339.115.
(11) “Unaccompanied youth” means a homeless student who is not in the  physical cus-
tody of a parent or guardian.
SECTION 3.
(1) Each district school board shall adopt a policy  that provides for the im-
mediate enrollment, school placement and provision of services to homeless students.  A
policy must provide for:
(a) The immediate enrollment, school placement and provision of services regardless of:
(A) Lack of records normally required for enrollment, including student education  re-
cords, immunization  or other health records, proof of residency, guardianship records or
evaluations for special services or programs;
(B) Any missed deadlines related to enrollment, school placement or the  provision of
services; or
(C) Any disputes related to the enrollment, school placement or provision of services.
(b) An obligation by the school  district to assist with the transfer of any required records
of a homeless student, including:
(A) Immediately requesting the records from the school last attended by the student;
(B) Ensuring the transfer of records in a timely manner to any subsequent school at-
tended by the student; and
(C) Directing the local liaison to assist with  any immunization or health record require-
ments.
(c) The identification and removal of barriers to accessing  academic and extracurricular
activities by homeless students.
(d) The placement of homeless students in schools in a manner that is consistent with:
(A) The provisions of this section; and
(B) For public schools attended voluntarily and for which there  is no attendance bound-
ary, the requirements for placing similarly situated students who are  not homeless students
in those schools.
(e) A  dispute resolution process consistent with the provisions of section 4 of this 2026
Act.
(f) The coordination with local social services  agencies and other agencies or entities
providing services to homeless students and their families,  including coordination with the
Department of Human Services for runaway and homeless youth under ORS 417.799.
(g) The  collaboration with other school districts to coordinate transportation, the trans-
fer of education records and the provision of access to extracurricular activities for homeless
students.

(h) The collaboration with any public charter schools sponsored by the school district  or
located within the school district to:
(A) Enroll or  place homeless students in the public charter school in a manner that is
consistent with the enrollment or placement of  similarly situated students who are not
homeless students;
(B) Provide services  available through the school district to homeless students placed in
a public charter school, including access to a local liaison and use of the dispute resolution
process described in section 4 of this 2026 Act; and
(C) Transfer any required records of a homeless student in a manner that is consistent
with subsection (1)(b) of this section.
(2)(a) Each school district shall designate at least one person to serve as a  local liaison
for the purposes of:
(A) Ensuring the effective delivery  of services for homeless students in the school dis-
trict, including:
(i) Identifying homeless students;
(ii) Assisting homeless students with enrollment and school placement; and
(iii) Assisting homeless students with accessing services  available for homeless students
under state and federal law;
(B) Aligning efforts to stabilize  and support homeless students and families in the school
district;
(C) Assisting unaccompanied youth with enrollment,  school placement, services for
homeless students and  the removal of other barriers to education and participation in ex-
tracurricular activities;
(D) Coordinating with administrators of  other programs serving homeless students in the
school district;
(E) Coordinating with any public  charter schools sponsored by the school district or lo-
cated within the school district; and
(F) Coordinating with the state coordinator as provided by section 5 of this 2026 Act.
(b) Nothing in this subsection prohibits a public  charter school from designating a person
to serve as a local liaison for  homeless students placed in the public charter school. A person
designated as a  local liaison by a public charter school shall coordinate with any persons
designated as a local liaison by the school district.
(3) Except as provided by subsection (4)  of this section, a school district shall place a
homeless student as follows:
(a) If the student had a school of origin, allow the  student to continue the student’s ed-
ucation in the school of origin for the duration of the time that the student is  a homeless
student, including continuing in the school of origin:
(A) For  the remainder of the school year during which the student became a homeless
student; or
(B) For the school  year following the summer during which the student became a
homeless student.
(b) If the student no longer is a homeless student during a school  year, allow the student
to continue the student’s education in the school that the student attended while a  homeless
student for the remainder of the school year.
(4)(a) A  school district may place a student in a different school than the school identi-
fied in subsection (3) of this section if the  school district determines that the placement is
in the student’s best interest.
(b) When determining a student’s best interest, the school district shall:
(A) Presume that keeping the student in the school of  origin is in the student’s best in-
terest, except when a parent, a guardian or an  unaccompanied youth requests placement in
a different school.

(B) Consider factors related to the impact  of mobility on the student’s achievement, ed-
ucation, health and safety while giving priority to  a request made by a parent, a guardian
or an unaccompanied youth.
(5) When a school district places  a student in a school that is not the school of origin
or that is not the  school requested by the parent, guardian or unaccompanied youth, the
school district must:
(a) Provide to the parent, guardian  or unaccompanied youth a written notice of the de-
termination that includes:
(A) An explanation of the determination, including specific reasons for the decision;
(B) The findings and  evidence relied upon for the determination and the sources of the
findings and evidence;
(C) Notice of the right to appeal the determination;
(D) A clear description of the  appeals process, including the dispute resolution process
described in section 4 of this 2026 Act, and the relevant timelines; and
(E) For an unaccompanied youth, contact information for the  local liaison with a brief
description of the liaison’s role.
(b) Direct the  local liaison to ensure that an unaccompanied youth receives the written
notice described in paragraph (a) of this subsection.
(6)(a) A homeless student enrolled in a  school of origin must be provided transportation
to and from:
(A) The school of origin, if requested by the student’s parent  or guardian or, for an un-
accompanied youth, the request of the local liaison; and
(B) Extracurricular activities to  the extent the transportation removes the barrier to full
participation in the activities.
(b) Transportation described in paragraph (a) of this subsection must be provided as  fol-
lows:
(A) If the student continues to live within the school  district where the school of origin
is located, the school district where the school of origin is  located must provide transporta-
tion.
(B) If the student moves to a different school district than the school district  where the
school of origin is located but remains enrolled in the school of origin, the two school dis-
tricts shall:
(i) Share the costs  and responsibility of transportation based on an agreement between
the school districts; or
(ii) Equally share the costs  and responsibility of transportation if the school districts
cannot agree on how to share the costs and responsibility of transportation.
(c) A homeless student enrolled in a school of  residence or in a public charter school
must be provided transportation to and from:
(A) The school of residence or the public charter school if:
(i) The transportation would be provided to  a similarly situated student who is not a
homeless student; or
(ii) The transportation removes the barrier to the enrollment, attendance  or success in
school of the homeless student; and
(B) Extracurricular activities to  the extent the transportation removes the barrier to full
participation in the activities.
SECTION 4.
(1) Each district school board shall adopt a dispute resolution  process for
when a dispute arises  over the enrollment, school placement or provision of services to
homeless students.
(2)(a) If a dispute arises  over a student’s eligibility as a homeless student or over a
homeless student’s school placement, the student shall be:
(A) Immediately enrolled in the school  in which enrollment is sought by the parent,
guardian or unaccompanied youth; and

(B) Provided transportation to and from the school and  extracurricular activities to the
extent the transportation removes the barrier to full participation in the activities.
(b) The  enrollment and provision of transportation described in paragraph (a) of this
subsection shall continue until final resolution of the dispute, including  all available appeals.
(3)(a) The school district shall establish a dispute resolution process and prescribe
timelines for the process to facilitate the expeditious resolution of  disputes. Any disputes
about school placement shall  include determinations of the student’s best interest, as de-
scribed in section 3 (4) of this 2026 Act.
(b) The dispute  resolution process shall include an optional informal resolution process
that may be used as  an alternative to a formal appeals process. The informal resolution
process must not waive the parent’s, guardian’s or unaccompanied  youth’s access to the
formal appeals process if the informal resolution process is not successful.
(c) Parents, guardians and unaccompanied youth shall have the right to bring a lawyer,
a nonlawyer advocate or any other person to provide assistance in the  dispute resolution
process.
(d) The school district shall provide translation, interpretation or other support services,
without charge, to  parents, guardians or unaccompanied youth who are involved in a dispute
resolution process and:
(A) Who are English language learners;
(B) Whose primary language is a language other than English; or
(C) Who need additional supports because of a disability.
(e) The  local liaison shall carry out the dispute resolution process as expeditiously as
possible after  receiving notice of the dispute. The local liaison shall assist the parent,
guardian or unaccompanied youth in preparing any appeals  and shall make the resources of
the school available to the parent, guardian or unaccompanied youth.
(4) The school district shall provide a written notice  of the determination of the dispute
resolution process to the parent,  guardian or unaccompanied youth. The notice must include:
(a) An explanation of the determination, including the specific reasons for the decision;
(b) The findings and evidence relied upon for the determination and the  sources of the
findings and evidence;
(c) Notice of the right to appeal the determination;
(d) A clear description of the appeals process, including relevant timelines;
(e) Contact information  for the local liaison and state coordinator, with a brief de-
scription of their roles; and
(f) A list of legal and advocacy providers  in the area that can provide additional assist-
ance.
SECTION 5.
The Department of Education shall designate a  state coordinator to be re-
sponsible for overseeing the implementation of sections 2 to 5  of this 2026 Act. The state
coordinator shall:
(1) Collaborate with educators, social  service providers and advocates for homeless stu-
dents and families in statewide programs and initiatives to address the needs of  homeless
students.
(2) Facilitate coordination between the department and  health care, social service and
other relevant service providers to ensure comprehensive support for homeless students and
their families, including developing an expedited referral system and interagency agreements
for sharing relevant data that is consistent with relevant privacy laws.
(3) Provide technical  assistance to, and conduct monitoring of, school districts in coor-
dination with local liaisons to:
(a) Coordinate with administrators of other programs serving homeless students  to en-
sure effective service delivery and to align efforts to stabilize and support homeless students
and families;

(b) Coordinate with early childhood education programs to ensure access to  appropriate
services for homeless children who are eligible for preschool; and
(c) Ensure that school districts comply with state and federal  laws related to homeless
students.
(4) Respond to inquiries  from parents, guardians and unaccompanied youth to ensure
that homeless students receive the full protections of state  and federal law and relevant
services related to homeless students.
(5) Coordinate with state and local housing agencies responsible for  developing a com-
prehensive housing affordability strategy for the  purpose of minimizing educational dis-
ruption for homeless students.
(6) Help ensure that the department and school districts  adopt policies and practices that
do not cause stigmatization or segregation of homeless students on the basis of  their status
as homeless.
