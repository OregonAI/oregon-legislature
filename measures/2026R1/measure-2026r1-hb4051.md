## Full text

83rd OREGON LEGISLATIVE ASSEMBLY--2026 Regular Session
House Bill 4051
Sponsored by Representative RESCHKE, Senator DRAZAN; Representative SCHARF (Presession filed.)
SUMMARY
The following summary  is not prepared by the sponsors of the measure and is not a part of the body thereof subject
to consideration by  the Legislative Assembly. It is an editor’s brief statement of the essential features of the
measure as introduced. The statement includes a measure digest written in compliance with applicable readability
standards.
Digest: The Act would have loans made to first-time home buyers to  offset closing costs. The
Act would make the loan plus interest due when the home is sold, the borrower moves out or, if the
home is a mobile home, it is moved out of state. The Act would  have the program run for three
years. (Flesch Readability Score: 88.1).
Creates a program for deferred loans to first-time home buyers in an  amount not greater than
the lesser of the closing costs on the purchase or one percent of the purchase price. Makes the de-
ferred loans repayable when  the home changes ownership, is no longer the homestead of the bor-
rower or, if the home is mobile, is moved out of state. Retires the program after three years.
Declares an emergency, effective on passage.
A BILL FOR AN ACT
Relating to first-time homeownership; and declaring an emergency.
Be It Enacted by the People of the State of Oregon:
SECTION 1.
(1)(a) Deferred loans shall be made in accordance  with subsection (4) of this
section to individuals determined to be  eligible under subsection (2) of this section in an
amount equal to the  lesser of the actual closing costs on the purchase of a homestead, as
defined in ORS 311.666, by the individual and one percent of the purchase price  of the
homestead.
(b) Deferred loans shall accrue interest at a rate of six percent  simple interest per an-
num from the date on which the loan is made.
(2) An individual is eligible to claim a deferred loan if the individual:
(a) Purchases a homestead located in Oregon;
(b)(A) Individually owns the fee simple estate of the homestead under a recorded instru-
ment of sale; or
(B) If two or more owners live in the property and all  owners file a claim jointly, owns
the fee simple estate with  rights of survivorship under a recorded instrument of sale with
all other claimants;
(c) Has never previously owned a homestead in any jurisdiction;
(d) Purchases the homestead at a price that is not greater than the  county median RMV,
as defined in ORS 311.666;
(e) Pays at least three percent of the purchase price in cash;
(f) Has insured the homestead for fire and other casualty; and
(g) Has a household income and net worth that are within the limits established under
ORS 311.668 (2).
(3)(a) Within 180 days following the  purchase of a homestead, an individual seeking a
deferred loan must file a claim with the Department  of Revenue that includes documentation
demonstrating to the department’s satisfaction the individual’s eligibility for the loan.
NOTE: Matter in  boldfaced type in an amended section is new;  matter [ italic and bracketed] is existing law to be omitted.
New sections are in  boldfaced type.
LC 233
(b) A claim must be signed by the individual, subject to the penalties for false  swearing
under ORS 162.075.
(c) The department shall establish by rule a timeline  and other matters necessary for the
filing and review of claims.
(d) The department shall review all claims in  a timely manner and notify the claimant
of the acceptance or rejection of the claim.
(e) An individual aggrieved by the  rejection of a claim may appeal the rejection to the
Oregon Tax Court within the time and in the manner provided in ORS 305.404 to 305.560.
(4)(a) Claims shall be granted on a first-come, first-served basis  based on the date on
which the claim was filed.
(b) Notwithstanding paragraph (a) of this subsection,  no more than 500 deferred loans
may be made for any calendar year.
(5)(a) The deferred loans shall be made from moneys in the Senior Property Tax Deferral
Revolving Account established under ORS 311.701.
(b) The department may make deferred loans on a quarterly basis.
(6)(a) The receipt of a deferred loan gives rise to a  lien against the homestead in the
amount of the loan, including interest accrued  under subsection (1)(b) of this section and any
penalties imposed under subsection (8)(c) of this  section, less any amounts paid in accord-
ance with subsection (7) of this section before the amount  becomes due under subsection (8)
of this section.
(b) A lien arising under this section has the same priority  as liens created under ORS
311.679.
(7) All or part of the amount of a deferred loan,  including accrued interest, may be paid
without penalty at any  time before the amount becomes due under subsection (8) of this
section.
(8)(a) Except as provided in paragraph (c) of this  subsection, the amount of a deferred
loan, including accrued interest, becomes due when:
(A) The homestead to which the deferred loan relates is sold, or a contract  to sell is
entered into, or some person other than the  individual who entered into the deferred loan
agreement, including a transferee, as defined in ORS 311.666, becomes the owner of the
property.
(B) The property is no  longer the homestead of the individual who entered into the de-
ferred loan agreement, except in the case of an individual who is required  to be absent from
the homestead by reason of health or military service.
(C) If the homestead is a manufactured structure or floating home, the homestead is
moved out of this state.
(b)(A) If a circumstance listed in paragraph  (a)(A) or (B) of this subsection occurs, the
amount described in paragraph (a) of this subsection becomes due and  payable to the de-
partment on or before August 15 of the year following the calendar  year in which the cir-
cumstance that triggers the payment occurs.
(B) If the circumstance listed in paragraph (a)(C) of this subsection  occurs, the amount
becomes due and payable five days before the date on which the  homestead is removed from
this state.
(c)(A) If an individual  willfully makes a false statement or misrepresentation, or willfully
fails to report a material fact, to obtain a deferred loan under this section, the  amount of
the deferred loan, including accrued interest and any penalties imposed under this paragraph,
becomes immediately due and payable.
(B) The department may  impose a penalty not to exceed five percent of the principal
amount of the deferred loan for the false statement,  misrepresentation or failure to report
a material fact.
(9)(a) If an amount that  becomes due under subsection (8) of this section is not paid
within 90 days following the due date, the amount shall be deemed delinquent as of the due
date and the lien against the property shall be subject to foreclosure as if it were a purchase
money mortgage under ORS chapter 88.
(b) In a foreclosure action under this section, a court may award:
(A) Reasonable attorney fees to the prevailing party.
(B) All costs associated with foreclosure of the lien to the department if  the department
prevails.
(10) All amounts paid under this section, including receipts from  foreclosure proceedings,
shall be transferred to the Senior Property Tax Deferral Revolving Account.
(11) The department shall publish information about the  deferred loan program, including
the eligibility requirements, claim process and  terms of deferred loans, on the department’s
Internet website.
(12) The department may adopt any rules it considers necessary or convenient  for ad-
ministering the deferred loan program established under this section.
SECTION 2.
(1) Deferred loans may be made under section 1 of this 2026 Act for home-
steads purchased in calendar years 2026, 2027 and 2028.
(2) Notwithstanding any timelines established under section 1 (3) of this  2026 Act, claims
for a deferred loan with respect to a homestead purchased in calendar year 2026 may be made
at any time after the effective date of this 2026 Act and on or before December 31, 2026.
(3) The repeal of section 1 of this 2026 Act by section 3 of this 2026  Act is not a circum-
stance that triggers repayment of a deferred loan under section 1 (8) of this 2026 Act.
SECTION 3.
Sections 1 and 2 of this 2026 Act are repealed on January 2, 2030.
SECTION 4. This 2026 Act being necessary for the immediate preservation of the public
peace, health and safety, an emergency is declared to exist,  and this 2026 Act takes effect
on its passage.
